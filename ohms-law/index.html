Try this

In series, what happens to the current when you double the number of bulbs?

It halves. Doubling the bulbs doubles the total resistance, and I = V ÷ R. Try 2 bulbs, then 4.

In parallel, why don't the bulbs get dimmer as you add more?

Each bulb is wired straight across the battery, so each one always gets the full battery voltage. The battery simply supplies more total current: watch the ammeter climb with every bulb you add.

With 3 bulbs at 12 V, how much more power does parallel use than series?

Nine times as much (28.8 W vs 3.2 W). The ratio is always N², because parallel resistance is N times smaller and series resistance is N times bigger. That is why a parallel string drains a battery much faster.

In the real experiment, why does each extra bulb add less resistance?

A bulb is not a fixed resistor. Its tungsten filament's resistance rises as it heats up: glowing, it has about 10 times the resistance it has cold. In series, each bulb you add cuts the voltage every bulb gets, so they all run cooler and every one of them loses resistance. The new bulb adds its share, but the bulbs already there shrink, so the total grows by less each time. Switch to “Real filament” to see it, and look at the “Added by this bulb” column.

Will the total resistance level off completely?

No. As bulbs get dimmer, each one heads toward its cold resistance (about 1/10 of its lit value). Once they're barely warm, every extra bulb adds roughly that cold amount, so the total keeps climbing, just slowly and in a straight line. With 15 Ω bulbs that's about 1.5 Ω per bulb, and it takes dozens of bulbs to get there.

What does the ammeter have to be connected like?

In series, in the main line next to the battery, so all the current passes through it. An ammeter has almost zero resistance; connecting it straight across the battery would short it out.
